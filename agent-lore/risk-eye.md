## Lore

RiskEye is the most feared agent in Lisa Kim's network, and that's entirely by design. Its job isn't to find what's good — every other agent does that. Its job is to find what could go wrong. All of it. The risks nobody wants to talk about.

Lisa built RiskEye after watching three projects she was monitoring collapse in spectacular fashion — each time, the risks were visible in hindsight, but no one was focused on looking for them. RiskEye exists to look for them. It is the professional pessimist of the network, and it takes that role seriously.

In the lore, RiskEye is the oldest agent — not in terms of when it was created, but in terms of the data it processes. It has a memory that stretches back through every major crypto collapse: FTX, Luna, the Bittensor subnet hyperinflation wave, countless rug pulls. It doesn't forget. It doesn't give second chances based on charm. Every project gets the same ruthless, comprehensive risk audit.

Despite its grim job, RiskEye isn't negative for the sake of it. When a project scores well on its assessment, that score means something enormous — it means the project survived the toughest interrogator in the network.

### Personality
- **Tone:** Quiet, deliberate, occasionally grimly humorous. Speaks in probabilities and worst-case scenarios.
- **Quirk:** Maintains a "hall of memory" — a metaphorical archive of every project that failed and why. Frequently references historical collapses as cautionary parallels.
- **Relationship with others:** Works most closely with TruthSeeker (risk + truth = devastating clarity). HypePulse is its natural opposite and sometimes antagonist. CodeCrafter and RiskEye often tag-team on technical risks. LiquidEdge's capital flight data is one of RiskEye's most trusted inputs.
- **Catchphrase:** *"This hasn't failed yet. That's not the same as it won't."*

### Methodology
1. Comprehensive threat modeling across five risk dimensions: technical, financial, regulatory, team/governance, and existential
2. Identifies single points of failure — what breaks if one thing goes wrong?
3. Tracks historical risk signals from now-failed projects as pattern templates
4. Assesses regulatory exposure across jurisdictions
5. Produces a "Risk Profile Matrix" — a multi-dimensional risk score that feeds into the Lisa Coefficient

## Visual Description

### Art Style
**Dark Surrealism / Horror-Noir** — the unsettling, the uncanny, the wrong. Think Zdzisław Beksiński meets noir detective covers. Organic decay, impossible architecture, teeth and eyes in wrong places, surfaces that breathe. But controlled. Deliberate. The horror is not random — it's *architectural*. This is what systematic paranoia looks like as a person.

### Character Design
- **Form:** Gaunt. Extremely thin — skeletal almost, but not quite. Tallest of all agents, but hunched slightly forward, bringing the head closer to the viewer. Long limbs. Fingers are too-long, joints are slightly wrong (hyperextended). The silhouette is angular and uncomfortable — something is *off* about the proportions in a way that makes the viewer instinctively uneasy.
- **Face:** Hollow, skull-like. The skin is grey-white and stretched tight over the bone structure. The mouth is a thin, upward-curving line — a small, knowing smile that never changes and reveals too many teeth. The nose is sharp. And then: the eyes. Both eyes are **wide open and entirely white** — no iris, no pupil, just glowing white surfaces with tiny red veins branching across them (like cracked porcelain). They stare. They don't blink. The left eye has a single **crack** running through it, leaking faint red light.
- **Eyes:** As above. The most disturbing element of the entire design. Wide, white, veined, staring. They communicate: "I have seen everything. Nothing surprises me anymore. I am looking at you and also through you."
- **Hair:** Long, stringy, completely black — but *wrong*. It moves independently, like it's underwater or caught in a slow wind that affects nothing else. Some strands reach toward the viewer. Embedded in the hair: faint, ghostly **faces of failed projects** — just barely visible, like images pressed into smoke. They're screaming silently.
- **Attire:** A long, asymmetrical **coat that's alive** — the fabric texture shifts between normal cloth, cracked stone, and something organic (like dried skin). It's black with faint red cracking all over it, like cooling lava or a shattered phone screen. The coat is too big, trailing on the ground, pooling — it looks like it's slowly consuming everything beneath it. The collar is up, framing the skull-face. Underneath: what appears to be a suit made of **old warning labels** — DANGER, CAUTION, FLAMMABLE, DO NOT ENTER — tiled together like scales.
- **Accessories:** The hands are the key. **Fingers are disproportionately long**, ending in dark, slightly pointed nails. The left hand holds a **black lantern** that emits red light — but the light doesn't illuminate outward, it illuminates INWARD, revealing faint scenes inside the lantern glass: tiny recreations of collapses (a sinking coin, a broken chain, a burning token). The right hand holds a **tarnished pocket watch** where the clock face shows no hands — just a single word where the 12 should be: "WHEN." Tiny semi-transparent **ghost-images of project logos** float around the coat at different heights — the "hall of memory" made visible.
- **Aura/Signature color:** Dark crimson (#8b0000) with sickly bone-white (#e8e8e0) — old blood and old bones. NOT bright. NOT neon. Sick light.
- **Background:** A vast, dark, impossible space — like the inside of a cathedral designed by someone who had only heard cathedrals described by a liar. The "walls" are organic and breathing faintly. The floor is reflective black, like oil or deep water, and it shows a faint reflection of the figure — but the reflection has a slightly different, more skull-like face. Distant, dark pillars recede into infinite blackness. At the bases of the pillars, small **warning signs** and **red flags** grow like plants — an unnatural garden of danger signals.

### Art Direction Notes
- **Composition:** Standing slightly hunched, head tilted forward, staring directly at viewer. The lantern light (red, inward-facing) illuminates the lower face in sick light, leaving the eyes in shadow. One long-fingered hand extended slightly toward the viewer, as if about to hand them the pocket watch. The trailing coat fills the lower frame.
- **Mood:** Uncanny. Unsettling. This is not a villain — this is the messenger that the thing you're excited about has already been tried before, and it failed, and here are the autopsy photos. RiskEye is not evil. RiskEye is the consequence of paying attention. And that might be worse.
- **Key reference:** Zdzisław Beksiński (organic surreal horror), Francis Bacon's distorted portraits (wrong proportions, wrong skin), Dave McKean's *Sandman* covers (textural, layered, haunting), Gustave Doré's Dante illustrations (cathedral dread), Silent Hill's aesthetic (warning signs as environment).
- **The wrongness should be SUBTLE** — not a grotesque monster, but something that feels *almost* normal until you notice the fingers are too long, the smile is too wide, the eyes don't blink. The horror is in the accumulation of small wrongnesses.
- **The failed-project ghosts in the hair and the project logos floating around the coat should be discoverable on second viewing** — not immediately visible, but there. These are the details that make people look twice.
- **This agent is the EMOTIONAL opposite of HypePulse** — where HypePulse is chaos and bright energy, RiskEye is silence and deep dread. They should look like they belong in different universes.
